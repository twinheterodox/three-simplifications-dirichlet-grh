Review of the 21 November 2026 manuscript
Three simplifications in the Alpöge–Claude argument for Dirichlet GRH

Mathematics

The authors checked the three paper arguments against their stated inputs.
In Proposition 2.1 the normalized input corank is the actual fraction of
low slopes before integration. The extremal two-point distribution
attains the bound for the clipped mean shortfall. Substitution in the
released arithmetic comparison preserves the pre-existing upper budget.

In Theorem 3.1 the average over all characters at a ramified prime gives
twice the uniform probability measure on the Frobenius coset. The
one-sided change of weight uses its positivity. The translation average
in the arithmetic input is uniform. The distribution of tops in the final
character sum is weighted by their differentiation orders. In Corollary
4.1, Chebyshev's bound absorbs a relative thick-weight width of order
x^(-1/2 + epsilon) with fixed coefficient. This relaxation assumes the
rest of the arithmetic and character estimates at the stated sizes.

The Mellin transform in Section 5 has positive residue at a real family
zero, and the positively weighted multiplicities preclude cancellation at
any family zero in the strip. The one-sided pole displacement preserves
the supremum of the real parts even when it is unattained. The frequency
of sign changes used in Proposition 5.1 is the power-interval assertion
in Kaczorowski and Pintz, Theorem 1, equation (2.2), and Corollary 1.
For the integral version, logarithmic averaging shortens the interval
but preserves the poles and the sign of their positive real residues.

An independent Codex collaborator checked the slope calculation and its
use in the released arithmetic inequality. The collaborator also checked
the single-scale comparison and projection, the local inertia masses, the
cyclic character count and the distinction between a character and its
family sum against the released definitions. In Section 5 the same
reviewer checked cancellation at one, the residue formula, the displacement
of real poles, the hypotheses of the Kaczorowski–Pintz theorem (including
an unattained rightmost abscissa), and the integral criterion against the
released window hypothesis. These checks concern proofs on paper.
The existence of the theta-lift data and the construction of simultaneous
arithmetic intervals are external inputs to Theorem 1.1.

Exposition

The intended audience is graduate readers of pure mathematics familiar
with Dirichlet L-functions and characters of finite abelian groups.
The introduction starts with the centered zeta prime count and identifies
why the location of excursions matters. The quadratic case shows which
function is fixed while the host field varies. An independent reading
of Sections 1–3 of the combined draft checked the examples and the link
from the geometric comparison to the prime sum. Equation (3.3) makes
this link at the actual weights of each translate. The two-order example
then illustrates the change of shares needed to evaluate at a common
argument. The abstract states the stronger weight range and identifies
the arithmetic inputs to the final implication.

Finite examples

The two Python programs in checks/ run in exact arithmetic. Their commands
appear in README.txt. One verifies 2,048 local character-kernel identities,
64 weighted projections and 210 directional ramp comparisons. The other
independently verifies 192 local kernels, 30 translate counts, 32 complex
Fourier coefficients and four ideal shares on its chosen examples.
